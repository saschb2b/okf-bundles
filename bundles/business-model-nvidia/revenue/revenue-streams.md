# One platform carries the business

NVIDIA reports two operating segments (Compute & Networking, Graphics) but discloses revenue by market platform, the more useful cut. In FY2026 (ended Jan 25, 2026) total revenue was $215.9B, up 65% YoY. [1]

| Platform | FY2026 revenue | Share |
|----------|----------------|-------|
| Data Center | ~$194B | ~90% [1][2] |
| Gaming (GeForce) | $16.0B (+41% YoY) | ~7% [1] |
| Professional Visualization | ~$3.2B | ~1.5% [2] |
| Automotive & Robotics | ~$2.35B | ~1% [2] |
| OEM & Other | ~$0.6B | ~0.3% [2] |

Source note: one source put full-year Data Center at $197.3B; the ~$194B figure is internally consistent with the $215.9B total, so treat Data Center as ~$194 to $197B. [1][2]

# Data Center is the engine, and still accelerating

The latest quarter, Q1 FY2027 (ended ~April 2026, reported May 20, 2026), set records: total revenue $81.6B (+85% YoY) and Data Center $75.2B (+92% YoY, +21% QoQ), beating consensus, driven by Blackwell Ultra plus InfiniBand, Spectrum-X, and NVLink. [3] Q4 FY2026 (reported Feb 25, 2026) was roughly $68B for reference. [4]

Inside Data Center, GPUs (Hopper, then Blackwell and Blackwell Ultra) dominate, and networking (InfiniBand, Spectrum-X Ethernet, NVLink) is the second pillar, a multi-ten-billion annualized run-rate though no longer broken out as a headline line. The buyers are covered in [customer segments](/market/segments.md), and how value is captured per deal in the [monetization model](/revenue/pricing.md).

# Software and services, the emerging line

NVIDIA AI Enterprise, Omniverse, and DGX Cloud are guided toward a multi-billion annualized run-rate, but NVIDIA does not disclose a clean standalone software revenue line, so exact figures are not verified. Growing this recurring layer is one of the [key strategic bets](/strategy/key-bets.md).
