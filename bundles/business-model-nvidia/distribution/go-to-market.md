# How NVIDIA reaches buyers

| Channel | What it is |
|---------|------------|
| Direct to hyperscalers | Large data-center deployments sold directly to the CSPs |
| OEM / ODM partners | Systems built and integrated by Supermicro, Dell, HPE, and Taiwan ODMs Foxconn/Hon Hai, Wistron, Quanta |
| Cloud partners | All major CSPs plus specialized GPU clouds (CoreWeave), which also host DGX Cloud |
| System integrators | Enterprise deployments |

These channels carry the [revenue streams](/revenue/revenue-streams.md) to the concentrated [customer base](/market/segments.md). The direct-to-hyperscaler motion is what makes the [customer concentration](/market/segments.md) both a strength and a risk.

# CUDA is a distribution channel, not just a moat

The CUDA developer ecosystem, on the order of 6 million developers (reported, approximate), functions as a demand-generation flywheel: developers build on CUDA, their software runs best on NVIDIA hardware, and that pulls their employers toward NVIDIA systems. This is the same asset that shows up as the central [moat](/moat/moats.md); on the sell side it is a go-to-market engine that no competitor can rent.

# The supply side

NVIDIA is fabless: silicon is fabricated by TSMC with advanced CoWoS packaging, and HBM memory comes from a small set of suppliers. That upstream dependency is the subject of the [cost structure](/economics/cost-structure.md), and it is the ceiling on how fast NVIDIA can actually ship what its channels sell.
