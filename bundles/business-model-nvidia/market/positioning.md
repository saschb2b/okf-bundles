# The claim

NVIDIA positions itself not as a chip vendor but as the platform for accelerated computing: the company that supplies the whole stack an AI build-out needs, GPUs, CPUs, networking, systems, and software, as one integrated, annually-refreshed product line. The pitch to a buyer is time-to-deployment and performance-per-watt at data-center scale, not a component.

# The position is near-monopoly, and contested

NVIDIA holds roughly 80% of the AI-accelerator market and over 90% of merchant data-center GPUs in 2026, with third-party analysts projecting share erosion toward ~75% as custom silicon scales. [1] Those share figures are third-party estimates and a moving range, not NVIDIA disclosures.

The dominance rests on the [moats](/moat/moats.md), chiefly CUDA and full-stack integration. The pressure on it comes from two directions covered there: AMD's Instinct line and the hyperscalers' own custom silicon (Google TPU, Amazon Trainium, Microsoft Maia, Meta MTIA), several of them built by the same [customers](/market/segments.md) NVIDIA depends on. Holding the position is what the [annual cadence and full-system strategy](/strategy/key-bets.md) are designed to do.
