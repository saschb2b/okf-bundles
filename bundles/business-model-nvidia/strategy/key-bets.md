# Six bets

## 1. An annual data-center cadence

Hopper to Blackwell to Blackwell Ultra (GB300, driving Q1 FY2027) to Rubin (Vera Rubin), with Rubin Ultra to follow; Vera Rubin systems are slated to begin coming online in the second half of calendar 2026. [1] The yearly rhythm is itself a [moat](/moat/moats.md): it forces rivals to chase a moving target.

## 2. Rack-scale, full-system selling

GB200/GB300 NVL72 as the unit of sale, moving NVIDIA up the value chain and lifting revenue-per-deal, the shift described in the [monetization model](/revenue/pricing.md).

## 3. Networking as a second platform

Spectrum-X Ethernet, NVLink, and NVLink Fusion (which opens NVLink to third-party and custom silicon, a hedge against the custom-silicon [threat](/moat/moats.md)).

## 4. Software and services as recurring revenue

AI Enterprise, DGX Cloud, and Omniverse, aimed at turning some of the hardware base into a subscription line, the emerging stream in [revenue](/revenue/revenue-streams.md).

## 5. Sovereign AI and physical AI

National-scale build-outs (Middle East, Europe, Asia) as a new demand category among the [customer segments](/market/segments.md), plus robotics and automotive (Isaac, Drive, Jetson Thor) positioned as the next large TAM.

## 6. Investing in its own demand

NVIDIA is deploying capital into the buyers and builders of AI compute, a flywheel that funds the customers who then buy its systems:

- **OpenAI:** intent to invest up to $100B, tied to ~10 GW of NVIDIA systems (first phase 2H2026 on Vera Rubin), announced Sept 22, 2025, though NVIDIA's CFO noted in Dec 2025 it was not yet a definitive agreement. [1][2]
- **Intel:** a $5B equity stake plus AI-processor collaboration, announced Sept 2025. [3]
- A growing investment portfolio (CoreWeave and others) tied to the same compute-demand flywheel. [4]
