# The four moats

| Moat | What defends it | Durability risk |
|------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Frontier capability | Best-in-class coding/agent models (Opus 4.x); Anthropic reports ~80% of its own code is Claude-authored [1] | Rivals close the gap each release cycle |
| Secured compute access | Multi-gigawatt deals: Amazon (up to 5 GW), Google/Broadcom TPUs (5 GW), SpaceX, memory partners [2] | Capacity is bought, not owned; a scarce-resource lease |
| Safety governance | Constitutional AI, PBC + Long-Term Benefit Trust as a trust signal [3] | Only a moat while buyers pay for trust |
| Enterprise trust | Governed-AI partnerships, HIPAA-ready tier, SSO/SCIM/audit [4] | Table stakes over time as rivals match |

# Capability and compute reinforce each other

Frontier model quality is what wins the [coding segment](/market/segments.md), and coding revenue funds the compute that trains the next frontier model. But compute is the binding constraint: Anthropic reportedly runs only ~1.5 to 2 GW of capacity today, which is why the gigawatt-scale [partnerships](/distribution/go-to-market.md) are existential rather than incremental, and why compute dominates the [cost structure](/economics/cost-structure.md). # Governance as a differentiator

The [safety-first positioning](/market/positioning.md) and the [Long-Term Benefit Trust](/strategy/governance.md) are marketed as reasons a regulated enterprise can trust Claude in production. Whether that is a durable moat or a temporary preference is the open question, and it is one of the [key strategic bets](/strategy/key-bets.md).
