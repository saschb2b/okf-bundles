# Compute dominates everything

The single largest cost is compute for training and inference. Analyst estimates put 2026 inference-plus-training spend near ~$10B, with compute running roughly 2.3x staff cost at AI-native firms (about $2M of compute per employee per year). [1][2] This is why the gigawatt-scale [compute partnerships](/distribution/go-to-market.md) are not optional: with only ~1.5 to 2 GW of capacity reportedly available today, capacity is the ceiling on both revenue and model progress. [2]

# Unusually few employees

Headcount is contested by source and method, ranging from ~2,300 (Fortune, Dec 2025) to ~5,000 (Tracxn, Mar 2026). [3][2] Either way, revenue-per-employee is extraordinary, a deliberate efficiency the company frames as a choice. Lean headcount keeps staff cost small relative to the [compute bill](/moat/moats.md) and against the [revenue run-rate](/revenue/revenue-streams.md).

# Margin dynamics (not officially disclosed)

- Gross margins are reportedly expanding as revenue scales against a compute base that grows slower than usage, helped by inference costs falling ~90% year over year. [4][1]
- Analysts caution the valuation embeds aggressive, unproven margin expansion (from roughly 40% toward 70%+). Actual current gross margin is not officially disclosed, so treat any specific figure as estimated. [4][1]

The [pricing mechanics](/revenue/pricing.md) (caching, batch discounts, the newer higher-token tokenizer) all bear directly on realized margin, which is why the margin story is inseparable from the pricing story.
